'@rentable/desktop': patch

a link alone opens nothing: every invitation and reset link now needs the six-character code that came with it before the organization is reached, and the code lasts as long as the link rather than ninety seconds

a signed-in member connects their own next machine: from their own machine they make a link and a six-character code, opening the pair on a machine that holds no organization connects it and lands at the sign-in wall where the same username and password admit them, and the link admits one machine once and lapses after seven days or when the credential it carries does, whichever comes first

the members section is now a directory of cards, one per person, the way every other record here is shown. a card names them, says their role, says how many workspaces they hold, and carries one line saying where they stand: no password yet, no machine signed in, or signed in on a machine. opening a card opens that person's details, and everything you can do to them is in the card's own menu, where an act you do not hold is simply not there. a link is offered only where the standing allows one, so the line that says somebody is signed in is also why there is no link to hand out. nobody changes their own role, permissions or workspaces, so a card you are looking at your own name on offers nothing, and neither does the owner's, whoever is reading it. the section's name, the sentence saying what it is for and the control that adds a member sit in a tray above the cards, the way the contracts list has it

the you section states facts and offers its writes on a surface: the password is a row saying what it is with a control that opens the three fields when you ask for them, rather than three empty fields drawn under a heading on every visit, and connecting another machine of your own sits under its own heading beside signing out of the others. the sync section no longer shows a link of any kind: the organization's own link is gone, along with the credential it carried that never expired, so nothing rentable makes reads your records without the code that came with it. an owner whose every machine is gone gets back in with their turso account and their password

the organization now knows which machines hold it: a machine joins the register when it connects, says who is signed in on it and when it was last seen, and leaves when it disconnects, so a machine that has not been seen for a week no longer counts as one of yours

the first screen of a machine that holds nothing now says what each way in needs: your turso account, for whoever owns the organization whether it is being made now or already there, and a link with its code, what an administrator or a member handed you. the connect screen is one form of both halves, the link and the six characters that came with it; an invitation then asks you to choose your password, a link you made for this machine connects it straight away, and a link that cannot be read and a code that is wrong are each said on their own field

an owner whose every machine is gone gets back in with their turso account. after the consent, an account that already holds an organization is no longer refused: the walk says so in a sentence, asks for the owner's username and password, and connects this machine to what is already there, signed in, with every credential in the organization renewed on the way. only the owner's password does it, and while a machine an owner or an administrator is on has been seen in the last week the walk says so instead and points at the link that machine can make

an owner can end the organization. under the turso account in the sync section, the owner alone is offered a delete: a panel says what goes, every workspace and everything in it and every member's way in, takes their password, and then removes every workspace database and the organization itself from their turso account and leaves this machine holding nothing. every other machine finds the organization gone the next time it opens and lands on the first screen. nothing puts any of it back

an account is made first and a link comes after it. the owner and administrators add an account from the members section with a username, a role, what the person may do and the workspaces they hold; it holds no password at all until its first link is opened. one act on the account makes that link and its code, and what kind of link it is follows the account: one whose password is not set yet asks the person to choose one when they open it, and one that already has a password lands its machine at the sign-in wall. a link is offered only while no machine is signed in on the account, so somebody who wants another machine signs out of the one they have first. resetting a password now takes the password away rather than handing out a link, and the next link asks for a new one. the second-machine control in the you section is gone with it: a member is given a link by whoever keeps the accounts

the workspaces section is now a directory of cards too, one per workspace, the way the members section reads. a card names the workspace and says how many people are in it, with a small dot before the name of the one you have open, and opening a card opens what you may change about it. rename, members and delete are in the card's own menu in plain words, and an act you do not hold is simply not there. the section's name, the sentence saying what it is for and the control that makes a new workspace sit in a tray above the cards, with the reason standing in the control's place where this machine cannot reach the turso account. export and import stay beneath, under the name of the workspace they act on

the owner can hand the organization to somebody else. on their own card in the members section, one act opens a panel saying what changes, the member they name becomes the owner and they become an administrator, and takes their password. nothing else about the organization moves: every record stays signed as it was, and the new owner gets back in on a fresh machine with their own password. the turso account does not go with it, so until the new owner connects their own account from the sync section the acts that need it run on the machine that connected the account first, and the sync section on their machine says so
